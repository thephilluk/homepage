{{< lead >}}
Hi. I'm Philip, a sysadmin from Hannover, Germany – and someone who stopped
meaning "I'll just have a quick look" literally a long time ago.
{{< /lead >}}

During the day I look after networks, servers and the things that are supposed
to work so nobody notices them. In the evening I do roughly the same again –
voluntarily, with worse documentation and considerably more cables.

## What keeps me busy

{{< columns >}}
**Networking and infrastructure.** Firewalls, VPN, routing, certificates. The
part of the job you rarely get praised for and always get called about.

**Programming.** Python for anything that should be automated. Lua for FiveM
servers. PHP, because it's there.
<--->
**Homelab.** An Unraid server, a Proxmox host, a few dozen containers and the
firm intention to tidy all of it up one day. This is where the notes end up
that I'll have to explain to myself next time.

**Gaming and simulation.** From Kerbal Space Program through Farming Simulator
to GTA roleplay. Mostly the kind of game where you read the manual voluntarily.
{{< /columns >}}

## Volunteering

I'm active at the [Hannover Tram Museum](https://www.tram-museum.de/) in
Sehnde-Wehmingen – where historic trams don't just stand around, but actually
run.

My role there is twofold. On the technical side: tools, apps and small systems
for museum operations – e-paper badges, radio concepts, digital bits and pieces.
On the operational side: as a regular member of the narrow-gauge railway crew,
I help make sure there's actually service on Sundays.

A surprisingly good combination: hundred-year-old technology on one side,
microcontrollers on the other.
